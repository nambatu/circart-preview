# Themed daily challenges: idea bank + review workflow

Admin (6 Nov 2026): "A challenge with only those pieces together with a story told along a red string would be amazing. Also daily challenges from museums or single artists together with their story. Give it for me to review every evening. Develop your own ideas."
Admin (6 Nov 2026, later): "if you have the time you can plan days ahead but the most important thing is the next day(s) and not next week or month."

## What a themed daily is
- **A handful of works with one red thread** (3 to 30 cards to place; "sometimes shortness is the sweetest"). Same for everyone that day, like today's dailies.
- **Intro** (2–3 sentences) before the first card: where we are, what connects the works.
- **A chapter for every card, the start card included** (shown with the reveal): it talks only about that card, never about the next one. On the results screen all chapters appear in year order and tell one story (Admin, 22 Nov; how to write them: `WRITING.md`).
- **Outro** on the results screen: how the story ends.
- **The order must not be chronological** (otherwise every card goes to the right end of the timeline): the red thread is a walk, a story or a theme, and the years jump. At most two rising years in a row.
- **At least six cards to place (Admin, 12 Nov):** fewer than six is generally not much fun.
- **The chapter is the only text players read after a card (Admin, 13 Nov):** story dailies hide the card trivia (roadmap task 53), so each chapter carries the story on its own. Make it red-lined and interesting: what happened, why it mattered, a surprising turn, and a lead into the next card. Leave out mundane facts (sizes, materials, which room it hangs in) unless they are part of the story. Still fill `trivia`/`trivia_de` (the Gallery and other modes show them), but never repeat it in the chapter.
- **Nude art is not explicit (Admin, 15 Nov):** "It's art." **Sexual scenes and titles are fine too (Admin, 24 Nov): never swap, leave out or flag a work for that.** Only mark `explicit: true` for what Admin calls explicit (like the Bacon cards).
- **Photos from the web are fine (Admin, 15 Nov):** if Commons has no photo of a work, use a good photo from another site (museum, auction house, a reliable art site) as `image_source`; the pack-images Action downloads any URL. Credit honestly.
- **Sources that disagree (Admin, 15 Nov):** take the better source and say so in the text, "according to …" (in `date_note`, or in the chapter for a story fact).
- **Uncertain dates need room (Admin, 7 Nov):** a card whose year is only approximate or attributed ("about 1240") is fine, but no other card in the story may be within about ±30 years of it.
- Writing style: the roadmap rules (plain sentences, no comma lists, at most one art pun per screen). EN + DE.

## Hard rules for every draft
- **Never worry about copyright (Admin, 17 Nov, standing rule):** any work can go in, with any good photo (Commons or another site). Don't flag copyright in drafts or reports; just fill `license` and `credit` with what the source says.
- Find photos with the `commons-lookup` workflow in this repo (cloud workspaces can't reach Wikimedia). Look at every downloaded image before the story goes live.
- Every year and fact needs a source (museum page or Wikipedia + one more). Leave out anything you can't source.

## Planning horizon
The next 1–2 days come first: build approved drafts for tomorrow / the day after into JSON and deploy them. Only then write a new draft, for a date in the next few days. Not every day needs a story.

## Build notes (learned 7–8 Nov)
- A story card whose id matches a pack card (same year + artist + title) gets the pack card's key and photo; the story upsert then overwrites that card's title/trivia in the database. Happened with Anguissola "The Chess Game" (Renaissance pack) and Morisot "The Cradle" (Impressionists pack) for 8 Nov. Harmless, but check the pack card's photo on the contact sheet.
- `publish_story.py` rewrites `supabase/dailies.sql` from the stories in `src/dailies/`. Every story stays in that folder, so earlier days stay in the file.
- In scheduled runs the web reader may be blocked (permission prompts nobody answers). Then use Commons descriptions from `commons-lookup` (they carry dates + museums) as one of the two sources.
- (8 Nov) In scheduled runs WebFetch worked for URLs that had come up in a WebSearch result first; other URLs failed with PROVENANCE_REQUIRED. So: search first, then fetch the result URLs.

- (11 Nov) A story card that matches a base-deck card (Mona Lisa, The Scream, Ghent Altarpiece) shares its key too. Copy the deck card's fields, English trivia and Commons photo exactly, so nothing changes for normal games.
- (11 Nov) `check_story.py` counts cards in a rising run: 1432 → 1814 → 1882 is already an error. Check the order of a draft with the tool before sending it to Admin.
- (12 Nov) In scheduled runs musee-orsay.fr pages can't be read (error from the site), and Wikipedia pages only if they came up in a search first. Google Arts & Culture, nga.gov, artic.edu and marmottan.fr worked.
- (13 Nov) Check the year of every card with two sources BEFORE sending a draft, not only at build: an approved card (Champaigne's skull) had to be swapped at build because no two sources agreed on its year.
- (13 Nov) No web reader in scheduled runs: pages are read through the `web-fetch` helper, which only opens URLs that came up in a search first (en.wikipedia.org links often don't; museum pages do).
- (14 Nov) Wikipedia's list of most expensive paintings only goes down to a certain price: cheaper records (Turner 2014, Rembrandt 2009) are not in it and have no today's-money figure. Check that when drafting a record-price story.
- (14 Nov) `commons-lookup` only finds files through categories. A painting without its own category (Turner's Aventine, Rembrandt's Arms Akimbo) turns up under "Paintings by <artist> in private collections" or "Paintings by <artist> in the <museum>".
- (15 Nov) Wikipedia pages open only if exactly that page came up in a search (the list of most expensive paintings did not; its mirrors lack the today's-money column). Commons has no photos of works still under copyright in the US (Kahlo, Beckmann, Picasso): check the photo before putting such a card in a draft.
- (16 Nov) Twins on Commons: check the catalogue number. Mondrian's "Composition No. II" sold in 2022 is Joosten B219 (51 cm); the Zürich picture is B217 (45 cm). The RKD's Mondrian catalogue (pietmondrian.rkdstudies.nl) lists both. A Commons file without its own category (Modigliani's "Amedeo Modigliani 012.jpg") can still be used: the thumb address is built from the MD5 of the file name, then check it on the contact sheet.
- (16 Nov) The `web-fetch` helper is expensive when given many pages at once (ten works in one go used a lot of the budget). Check facts at draft time, a few pages per run.
- (11 Nov) `gh` can't start workflows in scheduled runs; start `commons-lookup` with the GitHub connector (run workflow, input `category`).

A good pair: Cabanel's Birth of Venus and Olympia, both from 1863, but they can't both go in (two cards with the same year).

## Evening review format (one draft per evening, short)
| | |
|---|---|
| **Theme** | title EN / DE |
| **Red thread** | 1 sentence |
| **Suggested date** | in the next few days (why that day) |
| **Works** | year · artist · title · photo found (yes / not yet) |
| **Intro + 2 sample chapters** | to judge the tone |
| **Open questions** | anything Admin must decide |

Admin answers "approve", "change …" or "drop". Approved drafts are built into `src/dailies/<date>-<slug>.json` (full intro, chapters and outro in EN + DE) and deployed. Drafts waiting for an answer are kept in full in `src/dailies/drafts/`.
